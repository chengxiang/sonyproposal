# Independent review: How Representations and Weights Shape Multimodal Generation

**Assessment: weak positive; competitive for a Sony Focused Research Award shortlist.** The proposal presents a coherent mechanistic research project with a plausible creator demonstration. Its most valuable potential contribution is a tested correspondence between conditional information use, denoising progress, and reusable transformer computations. The main remaining weakness is that the procedure for selecting shared computations is less explicit than the procedure for selecting their inputs. Two narrower issues concern the editing ablation and confirmation of compute access. I identify three material criticisms below; none requires new experiments before submission.

**Independent reading basis.** I reviewed only the project materials in `/tmp/sony_independent_review_round3`: `proposal_draft.md`, `figures/fig1_mechanism_overview.png`, `figures/fig2_component_reuse.png`, and the 11-page `proposal.pdf`. I also read Sony's actual current call and consulted three targeted primary research sources to check the novelty boundary. This is a standalone review, not a comparison with another version.

**Fit and overall judgment.** Sony explicitly seeks mechanistic accounts of multimodal generation, including denoising dynamics, cross-modal binding, causal effects of model components, and interventions on internal representations. The proposed component–module studies and controlled scene variation directly address these interests. Knowledge externalization and attribution are additional interests in the call, not requirements that every proposal must cover; their absence here is not a deficiency. The one-year duration and USD 144,300 request fit the award's stated duration and funding ceiling. The PDF also fits the stated ten-page proposal-plus-references allowance followed by a one-page budget. **Strongest aspects.** First, the proposal has an understandable scientific object. A component is a fixed group of projected coordinates, not an undefined semantic factor, and locality is explicitly about component dependence rather than spatial proximity. Freezing the representation before dependency and schedule learning makes the subsequent comparisons interpretable. The proposal does not assume that PCA groups will become cleanly disentangled concepts.

Second, the masked-caption diagnostic anticipates important shortcuts. The word is removed before contextual encoding; full-caption target states remain at pure noise; the image-latent/backbone route is disabled; matched images share the incomplete caption. The frozen readout gives the conditional denoising task a semantic outcome. These are substantive controls already present, not missing work.

Third, the mechanism study goes beyond simply displaying attention maps. Component swaps, module removal and restoration, within-role swaps, magnitude controls, random-module controls, and checks on unrelated predictions can support a selective information-path account. Testing a predicted effect in an unrestricted checkpoint before imposing access restrictions is particularly valuable. The proposal also acknowledges the pretrained backbone's parallel path rather than claiming to explain the entire generator.

Fourth, the integration and creator demonstration are concrete. The image model retains its native corruption process and decoding path; the semantic branches have a stated parameter range; model-generated versus corrupted ground-truth semantic states will be compared. Editing starts from explicit forward corruption of source states and uses a revised complete prompt. The proposal permits coordinated changes across groups and necessary hand/arm movement instead of promising exact preservation from arbitrary coordinate groups.

Finally, the main evaluation is well aligned with the problem: all-requirement satisfaction, visually ambiguous roles counted as failures, independent role evaluation checked by blinded raters, paired quality assessment, prompt-level uncertainty, and reporting both conditional and end-to-end editing success. The stated preliminary results support the ingredients without being presented as completed proof of the new component–module correspondence. New award-period validation is the appropriate next step.

**Novelty check.** The comparison with Latent Forcing is substantially fair: that paper already examines noise-dependent cross-representation prediction, separate schedules, and output expert layers. Bradley already connects sparse conditional-score dependencies with compositionality and interventions, including feature-space analysis. Ge and colleagues already demonstrate causal cross-modal information routing in an image generator. Thus neither asynchronous representations nor causal routing alone establishes this proposal's novelty. The distinctive prospect is to use measured dependencies to make and test choices about reusable computations and controlled generation. That prospect remains credible, but makes criticism 1 important. This conclusion is an inference from a targeted comparison, not an exhaustive novelty certification. [Latent Forcing](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11401v1), [Local Mechanisms of Compositional Generalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16447), [Vision-Language Binding in In-Context Image Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24624)

**Ranked material criticisms**

1. **Specify how dependencies select a sharing hypothesis, and how that selection will be tested.**

   **Location:** Section 2.3, “Use measured dependencies to define module access” (PDF page 4): “We will implement a shared attention–MLP core with target-specific input/output projections, training it across different condition/target assignments and participant–attribute combinations.” Also Section 5's hypothesis that “Dependencies identify useful, reusable computations,” and Section 6's minimum deliverable of “one sharing hypothesis.”

   **Why it matters:** The proposal supplies a usable rule for selecting inputs: rank their measured benefits and retain a set meeting the 5% validation criterion. It does not supply a corresponding rule or one concrete prediction for deciding which conditional tasks should share the core. Two conditional predictors can depend on the same inputs yet require different functions. Conversely, successful multitask weight sharing can arise without the measured dependencies having identified that opportunity. The current small-model control permutes selected inputs while preserving the sharing architecture; that tests the value of input assignment, but does not separately test the basis for choosing the sharing arrangement. This is a missing specification at the central novelty claim, not an objection to shared modules or a demand for a theorem. The unrestricted-checkpoint intervention and unseen-composition tests already proposed remain valuable.

   **Proportionate revision:** Name one initial pair or family of conditional tasks and state the measurable dependency pattern that predicts compatible computation. Specify which core weights are shared and which projections may specialize. Within the existing small-model allowance, commit to a comparison that holds input access fixed while varying sharing—for example, the predicted pairing versus a dependency-uninformed pairing with matched sharing capacity, together with a suitable separate-predictor reference. State the outcome that would reject the sharing prediction. A short operational paragraph and one explicit comparison are sufficient; a general automatic module-discovery algorithm is unnecessary.

2. **The controlled-variation comparison does not yet isolate the value of dependency guidance from the flexibility of unequal noise allocations.**

   **Location:** Section 5, the paragraphs beginning “Visual groups that strongly inform the holder prediction…” and “Select a small set of component allocations and restart strengths…,” followed by “Creator-facing evidence” (PDF page 7). The named baselines are ordinary regeneration and a uniform-noise restart.

   **Why it matters:** Component-dependent restart has more allocation choices than a uniform restart, and those choices will be selected on validation cases. An improvement could therefore establish the usefulness of unequal noise allocation without showing that the measured conditional dependencies selected useful allocations. The existing matched-source design, fixed attempt limits, preservation scoring, and held-out evaluation address other important concerns, but do not remove this attribution ambiguity. The random input-access control in the small-model stage addresses a different intervention. This is an evaluation gap for the proposed dependency-guided control claim, not evidence that the editing procedure is invalid.

   **Proportionate revision:** Add one inference-only control using permuted or otherwise dependency-uninformed component rankings, with the same number of candidate allocations, validation-selection budget, restart-strength range, and sampling allowance. Tune the uniform restart under the same stated validation objective. Compare joint change-and-preservation success, or a small preservation-versus-change tradeoff, after freezing the choices. These cases can use the already planned source scenes; no additional full model training or pre-submission experiment is needed.

3. **Identify the source of the 5,000 H100 GPU-hours.**

   **Location:** Section 6, “Models, data, and compute” (PDF pages 8–9): “We estimate a total workload of approximately 5,000 H100-80GB GPU-hours.” Budget summary and basis (PDF page 11): the request is entirely personnel, fringe, tuition, and indirect costs, with no funding requested for other expenses.

   **Why it matters:** The run arithmetic is clear, includes frozen-backbone computation, and has a profiling milestone and reserve. What remains unknown is whether the required GPUs are already available through the laboratory, institution, or another allocation. A workload estimate does not establish access. Since the integrated comparison depends on this resource and the award request does not identify a compute expenditure, a reviewer cannot fully assess execution feasibility from the supplied proposal. This is a resource-documentation gap; it is not a finding that the budget is necessarily insufficient.

   **Proportionate revision:** Add a factual sentence identifying the existing or committed compute source and available allocation, and state that it covers the planned workload. If access is prospective, identify the acquisition route and the minimum experiment set supported by confirmed resources. Do not invent an institutional commitment or change the personnel budget without the relevant facts.

**Uncertainties that should remain research questions.** The PCA groups may carry overlapping role and preservation information; broader access or separate predictors may be needed; generated semantic states may lose the benefits measured with ground-truth features; and a holder change may require more source corruption than ideal for preservation. The proposal explicitly recognizes these possibilities and proposes relevant evaluation or fallbacks. I would not treat them as undisclosed methodological omissions. Likewise, restricting the committed demonstration to images and one relation is a sensible scope choice. An additional contemporary editing baseline would improve practical positioning, but is optional for this mechanistic award rather than a fourth material criticism.

**Funding assessment and priorities.** I would recommend serious consideration for funding, with a weak-positive rather than unconditional top-tier assessment. The theme fit is strong, the PI's reported work gives a relevant foundation, and the integrated question is worth pursuing. The proposal can produce useful scientific evidence even if sparse sharing fails, provided the sharing hypothesis is explicit enough to be falsified. The controlled scene-variation sampler is a credible research deliverable, although success at preserving all requested properties remains to be established during the award.

The highest priority is to make the dependency-to-sharing prediction and its comparison concrete. Next, add the inexpensive dependency-uninformed editing control. Finally, document compute access. These are targeted clarifications and award-period evaluation commitments; the proposal does not need a new pre-submission experimental campaign or a broader scope.
